## Description (4000 characters max)
```
Sharing a room is easy. Asking for it to yourself is the hard part.

Shorty turns "hey, could I get the room for a bit?" from an awkward, easy-to-avoid
conversation into a quick, low-pressure request -- so you actually send it instead of
just waiting around hoping your roommate leaves.

HOW IT WORKS
Pair up with your one roommate (Shorty is built for exactly two people sharing a room),
then request time whenever you need it. Study time, a phone call, some alone time, or
just "personal time" -- no reason required if you'd rather not say. Your roommate gets
the request instantly and can accept, decline, or counter with different timing.

QUICK REQUESTS, NOT FORMS
One-tap presets for the most common asks (15 minutes, 30 minutes, an hour), or set
custom timing down to the minute. No awkward back-and-forth text thread required.

STANDING ARRANGEMENTS
Got a class or a call that happens the same time every week? Propose it once. Once your
roommate approves, it repeats automatically -- neither of you has to ask again.

SEE EACH OTHER'S SCHEDULE
Import your class schedule straight from your phone's calendar or a photo of a printed
schedule, so your roommate can see at a glance when the room is naturally free --
sometimes a request isn't even needed.

NEGOTIATE, DON'T JUST DECLINE
Can't do the requested time? Counter with a different window instead of a flat no.
Quick +15 minute / +1 hour adjustments make it fast.

OPTIONAL: SWEETEN THE DEAL
If you want to, attach a price to a request -- some roommates like the option of paying
for guaranteed alone time. Shorty never touches the money itself; it just hands off to
Venmo, Cash App, Zelle, or Apple Cash so you can settle up directly with each other.

PRIVATE BY DESIGN
Shorty has no backend server of its own. Your data lives in your and your roommate's own
iCloud accounts, in a private zone only the two of you can see -- not on a server Shorty
runs, not sold, not shown to anyone else.

Built for roommates who'd rather send a two-second request than have another awkward
conversation about needing the room.
```

## Age Rating
Expect **4+** — no objectionable content categories apply (no violence, mature themes,
gambling, etc.). One thing Apple's questionnaire may flag: Shorty lets two paired users
exchange free-text notes with each other, which technically counts as messaging/UGC. In
the questionnaire, answer honestly that there's user-generated text content, but note
(where there's room to) that it's a closed, two-person-only exchange between people who
already know each other and chose to pair — not public content or contact with strangers.
This has not historically required App Review Guideline 1.2's public-UGC moderation
requirements (block/report/etc.) for closed two-person messaging, but if App Review asks,
that's the explanation to give.
